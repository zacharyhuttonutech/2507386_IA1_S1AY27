Three things that show up in school

These are the three traits I actually notice when school work is due, not a personality test result.

1. Curious

I am the type of person who can look at something I see every day and still want to know why it works the way it does. It does not have to be school, I will watch someone at the gym change their grip and then spend the walk home wondering what that changed. Same thing in a lecture. If the tutor moves on and I am still stuck on the first explanation, I do not like pretending I got it. I write the question in the margin and I go back to it later, because if I only copy the slide I forget it by the next class. The curiosity is annoying when I am short on time, but it is also the reason a topic sticks. I remember the parts I had to sit with.

2. Disciplined

The gym is where I learned this, not a classroom. There are mornings I do not feel like going and I go anyway, because the plan was already made the night before. School is the same habit with different clothes. I try to look at the work early in the week instead of discovering it the night it is due. I do not always win that argument with myself. The weeks I do show up early are the weeks I am not rushing and handing in something I would be embarrassed to read out loud. Discipline is what keeps the curious side from wandering. I can spend an hour on one question I like and leave the rest untouched. So I set a stopping point. Finish the part I said I would finish, then I can go back down the rabbit hole if there is time.

3. Creative

I get bored when everything has to look and sound the same, If I only rewrite a tutor's notes in the same order, my brain checks out. I do better when I can put it in my own layout, a messy page of arrows, or an example from my actual week in Portmore instead of the example on the slide. That is not about making it pretty. It is the only way the work feels like mine, and I stay with it longer when it feels like mine. When a course forces one format, I still do the format, then I keep a second page that is just how I understand it. That second page is usually the one I revise from before a test.

The person I look up to

My mentor at the gym

He is not a celebrity he is an older guy at my gym who was already training when I started, and who talks less than most people in the room. The background is ordinary. He works, he trains, he shows up on days he does not feel like it. That is the whole thing I respect. I do not know his life story in some documentary way. I know what he does when the set is hard and nobody is watching.

The line I still hear is: motivation comes and goes, discipline does not.

The scene I remember is a day I wanted to leave early. I told him I was not feeling it. He did not give me a speech. He told me to finish the last set, because how I felt was not the plan I had already made. I stayed. It was not dramatic. I was just annoyed, then I was done, then I was glad I did not bargain with myself.

School is the same room in a different building. Some nights I do not feel like sitting down with the work. If I only study when I feel motivated, the week disappears. So I use his line as the rule. Sit down. Do the part I said I would do. The feeling can catch up after.

LLMs as a tool, not a crutch

The statement means the chatbot can sit next to the work, it cannot be the work. ChatGPT, Claude, and Copilot will all produce a page if I paste the assignment brief. If I submit that page, I have a file and I still cannot explain a selector in the tutorial. For me that is the crutch. The mark is for what I can build and defend, not for what a model typed while I watched.

I am allowed to use it where I am stuck on a small question. Example: I could not remember why my image path failed when the HTML file was inside Codes. I asked, then I checked the folder myself, then I fixed the src to go up one level. That use made the learning faster. Asking it to “make my whole portfolio” and then changing the colours so it looks different is not faster learning. It is a disguise.

Where I refuse to use it is the first draft of my own reflection. The Babylon notes and this essay have to sound like the semester I am actually having, data money, lab files, a gym that has nothing to do with a case study. A model does not live in Portmore and it does not know which tag I keep forgetting. If I let it write those paragraphs I cannot talk about them in class without reading off the screen.

How I use it now, when I use it at all: one question, my own rewrite, then I open the page in a browser. I check HTML against MDN and the living standard when the answer feels too confident. UNESCO’s guidance says these tools should support a learner’s judgment and should not replace it (Miao & Holmes, 2023). That matches the rule I am trying to keep. The tool explains. I still have to be the one who writes the line and knows why it is there.
